Superposition

Swerdlow’s Fig. 1.2 does not show one model. It shows two — the eccentric and the epicyclic form of Ptolemy’s solar model — in one plane. That is a superposition:

# The two forms of Ptolemy's solar model in one plane: what Swerdlow's Figs. 1.2 and 1.3 draw.
# The superposition says only that both models are about the same earth, the same apsidal direction and the
# same mean motion. That the two suns coincide (Fig. 1.2) or lie on one ray (Fig. 1.3) is not said here: it is
# derived, under hypotheses on the radii, in solar-equivalence-equal-radii and solar-equivalence-proportional.
superposition solar-eccentric-epicyclic "Eccentric and epicyclic form of Ptolemy's solar model, superposed"
witness fr:kepler-sec/Swerdlow2026/0050/fig01 "Fig. 1.2"
witness fr:kepler-sec/Swerdlow2026/0051/fig01 "Fig. 1.3"
author "Gerd Graßhoff"
place solar-eccentric as Ecc
place solar-epicyclic as Epi
identify Ecc.O = Epi.O                 "one earth"
identify Ecc.lamA = Epi.lamA           "one apsidal direction"
identify Ecc.T = Epi.T                 "one year"
identify Ecc.kappa_0 = Epi.kappa_0     "one epoch"
identify Ecc.kappa_bar = Epi.kappa_bar "one mean anomaly"
display Epi.lam "λ'_S"
display Ecc.lam "λ_S"

Every name of a member gets the member’s prefix (Ecc.S, Epi.S'); the names the text identifies become one (O, lamA, kappa_bar). Identification says that the two models are about the same earth, the same apsidal direction, the same mean motion. It adds no geometry. Only inputs — parameters of one unit, kinematic variables — and the observer can be identified; a text that identifies two constructed points is refused:

identify Ecc.S = Epi.S': only parameters of one unit, kinematic variables and the observer can be identified;
that two constructed points coincide is a proposition to derive

The superposition is again a model: pure geometry over the parameters of both members. In it the two suns are two points. They coincide for some parameter values and not for others; nothing in the superposition says which.

Notes and gaps

  • parameters of one member only (free until a hypothesis or a parametrisation relates them): Ecc.R, Ecc.e, Epi.R’, Epi.r

Equivalence is a relation, derived

That the two forms are equivalent is a relation between the two models. It holds relative to hypotheses and to what is compared:

derivation hypotheses the models are equivalent in
solar-equivalence-equal-radii (Fig. 1.2) R′ = R, r = e position: the two suns are one point in every state
solar-equivalence-proportional (Fig. 1.3) R′/r = R/e direction: the sun is seen from the earth in the same direction; the distances differ

Each is the conclusion of a derivation in the superposition (Derivations), not something the figure asserts by drawing one point.

Identity is something else

Two texts are the same model when they are the same geometry up to renaming. diff tests that:

$ famulus-astra diff solar-eccentric solar-epicyclic
solar-eccentric ↔ solar-epicyclic: different geometries
  parameters
    − R
    − e
    + R'
    + r
  construction
    − E (point) = O + polar(e, lamA)
    − ecc (circle) = circle(E, R)
    …

The eccentric and the epicyclic form are different models that are equivalent under hypotheses. What stays different under those hypotheses is everything that is not compared: in the one the sun moves uniformly on a circle about E, in the other its motion is compounded of two.

Values and figures of a superposition

A parametrisation of a superposition takes over the values of a member’s parametrisation and adds its own:

# Ptolemy's values in the superposition, with the radii of Fig. 1.2: R' = R and r = e (p. 19).
parametrisation solar-eccentric-epicyclic.equal-radii of solar-eccentric-epicyclic "Ptolemy's values, R' = R and r = e"
